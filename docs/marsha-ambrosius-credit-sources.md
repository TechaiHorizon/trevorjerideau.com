# Marsha Ambrosius — portfolio credit sources

Trevor Jerideau confirmed his **album producer and A&R** roles on *Late Nights & Early Mornings* and *Friends & Lovers*. These roles are supplied by Trevor; the external sources below support the release details, charts, awards and nominations. No artist-signing claim has been added.

## Late Nights & Early Mornings — 2011

Marsha Ambrosius’s J Records solo debut was released on March 1, 2011. The album reached No. 2 on the Billboard 200 and No. 1 on Top R&B/Hip-Hop Albums, with 96,400 copies sold in its opening week. Its singles include “Hope She Cheats on You (With a Basketball Player),” which reached No. 22 on Hot R&B/Hip-Hop Songs, “Far Away” and the title track. The canonical album title uses an ampersand. [1] [2]

“Far Away” peaked at No. 3 on Hot R&B/Hip-Hop Songs and No. 74 on the Hot 100. It received two nominations at the 2012, or 54th, Grammy Awards: Best R&B Song and Best R&B Performance. These are song/performance nominations, not an album nomination or personal awards to Trevor. The Recording Academy’s current table retroactively displays “Best R&B Solo Performance”; the historical 2012 wording used in the artist biography and song article is “Best R&B Performance,” which is retained on the portfolio. [3] [4] [5]

“Far Away” won the **2011 Soul Train Record of the Year, also labeled the Ashford & Simpson Songwriter’s Award**. Contemporary VIBE coverage lists this as one combined award; it must not be counted as two separate wins. It was not the 2011 Soul Train Song of the Year winner. The video was nominated for Video of the Year at the 2011 BET Awards. BMI’s official 2012 Urban Music Awards list also includes “Far Away” among its award-winning songs, naming Marsha among the credited writers. This BMI recognition was not Songwriter of the Year or Song of the Year. [6] [7] [8]

## Friends & Lovers — 2014

*Friends & Lovers* was released in the United States on July 15, 2014 through RCA Records. It debuted and peaked at No. 12 on the Billboard 200 and No. 2 on both Top R&B/Hip-Hop Albums and the separate Top R&B Albums chart. Billboard’s contemporary report describes approximately 17,000 first-week copies sold and the album’s Hot Shot Debut distinction on Top R&B/Hip-Hop Albums. These are chart and sales highlights, not a certification or an awards-ceremony honor. [9] [10] [11] [12] [13]

“Run” peaked at No. 13 on Adult R&B Airplay and No. 44 on R&B/Hip-Hop Airplay. The No. 44 position belongs to **Airplay**, not Hot R&B/Hip-Hop Songs. Other highlights include the single “Stronger,” featuring Dr. Dre and reworking Sade’s “Stronger Than Pride,” “Spend All My Time” featuring Charlie Wilson, and “Streets of London” featuring Skye and Lindsey Stirling. No unsupported chart position is assigned to those collaborations. [9] [13] [14] [15] [16]

No Grammy award or nomination specifically for this album or its reviewed songs was verified in the Recording Academy history. Marsha’s 2015 nomination for “As” with Anthony Hamilton is unrelated guest work and has not been transferred to this album. Her Floetry honors and honors for later solo albums are also excluded from these two project credits. [4]

## Artist-level recognition

Marsha won the 2011 BET Centric Award. The archived official nominees list supports her 2011 Best Female R&B Artist nomination, and contemporary Hollywood Reporter coverage supports a separate nomination in the same category in 2012. Her 2011 Soul Train nominations included Best R&B/Soul Female Artist and Best New Artist. These are described as Marsha’s recognition in the artist introduction, rather than album awards or personal awards to Trevor. [1] [17] [18] [19]

The 2014 MOBO nominations were **Best Female Act** and **Best R&B/Soul Act** for Marsha. Although the album Wikipedia page describes two MOBO nominations “for the album,” its cited full nominations list identifies artist categories. *Friends & Lovers* was not nominated for Best Album, and the list does not nominate a song from the album for Best Song. The portfolio therefore places the two artist nominations in Marsha’s introduction, not in the album card as album nominations. [20] [21]

No relevant RIAA Gold or Platinum certification was found in the official artist and album-title searches reviewed. No certification claim or badge has been added. This reflects the reviewed U.S. database results, not a claim of exhaustive worldwide certification research. [22] [23]
